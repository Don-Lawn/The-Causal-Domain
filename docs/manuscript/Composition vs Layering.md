

# **Composition vs Layering**

## **1. Overview**

Physics contains two very different kinds of structure:

- **Layering** — adding new dimensions
- **Composition** — assembling structures within a layer

These are often confused, but the distinction is essential.

## **2. Layering**

Layering occurs when a new **primary axis** Pi is introduced. This creates a new symmetry layer and expands the dimensional structure.

Examples:

- space (L₁)
- charge (L₂)
- mass/curvature (L₃)
- internal indices (L₄)

Layering is rare and fundamental.

## **3. Composition**

Composition occurs **within** a layer. It does not introduce new dimensions; it introduces new **structures**.

Examples:

- quarks → proton
- electrons + nuclei → atoms
- atoms → molecules
- molecules → materials

Composition is ubiquitous and structural.

## **4. Why the Distinction Matters**

- A proton is not “higher‑layer” than a quark.
- An atom is not “higher‑layer” than an electron.
- A black hole is not “higher‑layer” than a star.

They are all **structures within the same layer**.

Layer transitions change the **rules**; composition changes the **arrangements**.
