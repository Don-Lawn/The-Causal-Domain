## [Gravitational Waves](Gravitational-Waves.md)

In the standard description, gravitational waves are oscillations in spacetime curvature that propagate at the speed of light. They carry energy, momentum, and stress, yet they have no rest mass and no material carrier. This makes them conceptually similar to electromagnetic waves, but with a crucial difference: the energy they carry is not stored in a field defined by charge, but in the **geometry itself**.

In the RR framework, gravitational waves arise naturally as **rotations between gravitational potential energy (GPE) and kinetic energy (KE)** within the L₃ curvature layer. This rotation occurs in the **P₃–S₃ plane**, with the **Q₃ axis** acting as the normal along which curvature depth and gravitational locks operate.
