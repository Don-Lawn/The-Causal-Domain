# **Symmetry Locks (Q‑Locks)**

## **1. Overview**

Every symmetry layer Li introduces a new degree of freedom in the Pi–Si plane. But the universe also introduces a corresponding way to **restrict** that freedom without eliminating the symmetry itself. This restriction occurs along the **Q‑axis**, the axis normal to the layer.

A **Q‑lock** is the mechanism by which a boundary, horizon, or internal constraint limits the expression of a symmetry.

Q‑locks are not failures of symmetry; they are **expressions of symmetry under constraint**.

## **2. The Structure of a Q‑Lock**

A Q‑lock has three defining features:

### **2.1. It acts along the Q‑axis**

The Q‑axis Qi is orthogonal to the symmetry plane. It is the axis that determines how far the symmetry can “tilt” or “rotate” out of its natural plane.

### **2.2. It restricts, but does not destroy, the symmetry**

A Q‑lock reduces the amplitude, range, or freedom of the symmetry. The symmetry still exists; it is simply constrained.

### **2.3. It is layer‑specific**

Each layer has its own type of Q‑lock, because each layer has its own Q‑axis.

## **4. Why Q‑Locks Matter**

Q‑locks unify phenomena that appear unrelated:

- EM exclusion in conductors
- gravitational time dilation
- color confinement
- SUSY breaking

All are **the same structural mechanism** expressed at different layers.
