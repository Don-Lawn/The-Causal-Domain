# Causal Domain as Hypercone

This model represents the causal domain as a hypercone: the unrolled, flattened
version of a single meridian of the causal hypersphere. It provides the working
coordinate system (q, w) used throughout the RR framework. The hypercone is the
operational model of causal depth, compactness, and projection into the
phenomenal domain.

Where the hypersphere encodes **rotation into Q**, the hypercone encodes **depth
resulting from that rotation**.

## 1. Purpose of the Hypercone Model

The hypercone is used because it provides:

- a **flat**, diagram‑friendly geometry,
- a **linear** q-axis representing causal depth,
- a **normalised** w-axis representing compactness,
- a **universal** shape shared by all gravitational wells,
- a **direct mapping** into physical radius \( r \).

The hypercone is the model used for calculations, diagrams, and projection into
the phenomenal domain.

---

## 2. Origin of the Hypercone

The hypercone is obtained by:

1. selecting a meridian on the causal hypersphere,
2. cutting the hypersphere along that meridian,
3. peeling the strip away,
4. flattening it into a 2D surface.

The curved geodesic of the meridian becomes a straight line. The equator becomes
the line \( w = 1 \). The pole becomes the apex of the cone.

This unrolling preserves the ordering of q-depth and the equivalence of all
meridians.

---

## 3. The q-Axis: Linearised Depth

On the hypersphere, q is an **angular** measure of rotation into Q.  
On the hypercone, q becomes a **linear** measure of depth.

Properties:

- q runs from 0 (pole) to 1 (equator),
- q is monotonic,
- q is universal for all wells,
- q encodes causal depth, not physical distance,
- q is the primary coordinate for stiffness \( S(q) \) and Tempo \( \tau(q) \).

The q-axis is the straightened meridian.

---

## 4. The w-Axis: Normalised Compactness

The w-axis represents the transverse direction obtained when the hypersphere is
unrolled. It measures **compactness** or **angular spread** around the meridian.

Properties:

- w runs from 0 at the pole to 1 at the equator,
- w = 1 corresponds to the equator of the hypersphere,
- all wells share the same w = 1 boundary,
- the hypercone has a universal base radius of 1 in w-space.

The w-axis encodes the fact that all wells overlap at maximum depth.

---

## 5. Geometry of the Hypercone

The hypercone is not a physical cone but a **causal geometry**. Its slope encodes
the curvature of the hypersphere. Its base encodes the completeness of all
possible wells.

Key features:

- apex at q = 0, w = 0,
- base at q = 1, w = 1,
- straight meridian lines,
- circular base representing the equator,
- universal shape independent of mass.

The hypercone is scale-free; physical size appears only after projection.

---

## 6. Mapping to the Phenomenal Domain

The hypercone maps into the phenomenal domain via the projection:



\[
(q, w) \;\rightarrow\; r
\]



This projection determines:

- physical radius,
- curvature in 3D space,
- gravitational redshift,
- geodesic behaviour.

The hypercone provides the causal depth profile; the phenomenal domain displays
its physical consequences.

---

## 7. Relationship to the Hypersphere

The hypercone is the **unrolled representation** of a hypersphere meridian.

- Hypersphere: rotation into Q  
- Hypercone: depth resulting from that rotation  

The equator of the hypersphere becomes the line w = 1.  
The meridian becomes the q-axis.  
The curvature becomes the slope.

For details, see `((Relationship)) Hypersphere–Hypercone.md`.
