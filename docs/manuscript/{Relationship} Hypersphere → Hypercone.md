# Hypersphere → Hypercone

This file describes the geometric and conceptual relationship between the causal
domain represented as a hypersphere and the same domain represented as a
hypercone. Both models describe the same underlying causal structure, but in
different coordinate languages.

The hypersphere encodes **rotation into Q**.  
The hypercone encodes **depth resulting from that rotation**.

A meridian on the hypersphere becomes the q-axis of the hypercone when unrolled.
The equator of the hypersphere becomes the line w = 1 on the hypercone.

---

## 1. The Hypersphere Meridian

A meridian on the hypersphere is a great-circle path running from the pole
(q = 0) to the equator (q = 1). Moving along this meridian corresponds to
**rotating the local orientation into Q**, increasing causal depth.

Properties:

- q is an **angular** measure of rotation into Q.
- The meridian is a **curved geodesic** on a closed surface.
- Every point in phenomenal space corresponds to one meridian.
- All meridians are equivalent by symmetry.
- The equator contains the **endpoints of all wells**.

The hypersphere is the **rotation model** of the causal domain.

---

## 2. The Hypercone Meridian

When a meridian is unrolled, the curved arc becomes a straight line. The q-axis
of the hypercone is therefore the **straightened version** of the hypersphere
meridian.

Properties:

- q becomes a **linear depth** coordinate.
- The transverse direction becomes the **w-axis**.
- The equator becomes the line **w = 1**.
- The geometry is **flat and normalised**.
- The hypercone is universal for all wells.

The hypercone is the **depth model** of the causal domain.

---

## 3. Unrolling the Meridian

Unrolling means conceptually cutting the hypersphere along a meridian and
flattening the resulting strip. When a curved geodesic on a sphere is flattened,
it becomes a straight line. The curvature of the hypersphere becomes the slope of
the cone.

The unrolling preserves:

- the ordering of q-depth,
- the causal descent,
- the equivalence of all meridians,
- the maximum depth at q = 1.

The unrolled meridian is the same causal path expressed in a different coordinate
language.

---

## 4. The Equator and w-Space

Each point on the equator of the hypersphere corresponds to the maximum depth of
a different gravitational well. The equator is therefore the **set of all well
bottoms**.

When unrolled:

- the equator becomes the circle **w = 1**,
- all wells overlap at w = 1,
- the base of every hypercone has radius 1 in w-space.

This mirrors the behaviour of real gravitational wells in the phenomenal domain,
whose 1/r tails overlap everywhere in space.

The equator is not a sum or transform; it is a **complete set of directions**,
analogous to a basis.

---

## 5. Mapping Between the Two Meridians

The hypersphere meridian encodes **rotation**.  
The hypercone meridian encodes **depth**.

The mapping is:

- angular q → linear q  
- curvature → slope  
- rotation → depth  
- equator → w = 1  
- meridian → q-axis  

The two meridians describe the same causal descent, but in different coordinate
languages.

---

## 6. The Causal Hierarchy

The transformation from hypersphere to hypercone expresses the causal hierarchy:

1. **Rotation**  
   Orientation into Q on the hypersphere.

2. **Depth**  
   Linearised q-depth on the hypercone.

3. **Volume / Radius**  
   Projection into the phenomenal domain.

This hierarchy underlies the entire RR framework.

This relationship file defines the conceptual bridge between the two causal

causal domain.
