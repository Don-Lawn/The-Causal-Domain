# **The Q‑Toroid: **

## **Minimal Stable Object of RR**

A stable physical object in RR arises when a Q‑layer inherits **two independent rotations** from its parent frame. These rotations originate in the two fundamental RR symmetries:

- **RSR** — the inherited *kinematic* rotation
- **RGR** — the inherited *gravitational* rotation

In ordinary 3‑space, these rotations cannot coexist independently. In RR, they do — because each Q‑layer possesses an additional degree of rotational freedom along the **Q‑axis**.

When both inherited rotations are active, the Q‑layer collapses into a **dual‑rotation toroidal structure**, the **Q‑toroid**.

## **1. Dual Rotations**

The Q‑toroid carries two closed rotational flows:

- **Toroidal rotation** (around the ring)
  - inherited from RSR
  - corresponds to momentum and phase rotation
- **Poloidal rotation** (around the tube)
  - inherited from RGR
  - corresponds to mass, confinement, and gravitational behaviour

These two rotations are orthogonal and independent. Their coexistence is the defining feature of the Q‑toroid.

## **2. Tempo and Confinement**

The local Tempo gradient

Θ=dtdT

creates a radial contraction toward Q‑depth. This contraction stabilises the dual‑rotation structure and selects **quantised radii** where the two rotations phase‑lock.

Thus, the Q‑toroid is not assumed — it is **forced** by:

- inherited rotations
- Tempo gradients
- Q‑axis geometry

## **3. Spin, Charge, and Chirality**

- **Spin** arises from the global phase winding of the toroidal rotation.
- **Charge** arises from the **handedness** of the dual rotations relative to the Q‑axis.
- **Chirality** is built into the geometry.

## **4. Relation to the Spinning‑Top Model**

The earlier spinning‑top analogy captured:

- a fast rotation (spin)
- a slow rotation (precession)

These map directly to the toroidal and poloidal rotations of the Q‑toroid.

The spinning‑top was a useful intuition, but the Q‑toroid is the **closed, quantised, stable** version of that structure.

## **5. Relation to Toroidal Electron Models**

Phenomenological toroidal models (e.g., Hüfner) assume:

- a toroidal current
- a poloidal circulation
- magnetic self‑confinement

RR provides the **geometric origin** of these features. The Q‑toroid is the underlying structure from which such models arise.
