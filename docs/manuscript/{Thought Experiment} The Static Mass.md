# The Static Mass

This thought experiment establishes the simplest possible gravitational scenario:
a single, isolated mass suspended in otherwise empty space. The mass is not
moving, not radiating, and not interacting with anything else. Its presence alone
distorts the causal domain, producing a static curvature profile that extends
outward indefinitely.

Nothing evolves in time. Whether three seconds or a million years pass, the
geometry remains exactly the same. The distortion is not a process but a state: a
fixed deformation of the causal domain that gives rise to the gravitational well
in the phenomenal domain.

This thought experiment defines the baseline geometry used throughout the RR
framework. All dynamic scenarios are built as variations or perturbations of this
static case.

---

## Domain Separation

### Causal Domain (k-domain)
- Higher-dimensional parent-field geometry  
- Contains the Q-axis (depth)  
- Contains stiffness \( S(q) \)  
- Contains Tempo \( \tau(q) \)  
- Contains the hypersphere representation  
- Static in this experiment  
- Not directly observable  

### Phenomenal Domain (x-domain)
- The 3D space we observe  
- Contains physical radius \( r \)  
- Contains curvature as measured  
- Contains gravitational redshift and time dilation  
- Contains geodesics  
- Displays the projection of the causal deformation  

The gravitational well we observe is the projection of a static deformation in the
causal domain.

---

## Assumptions

1. The mass is perfectly static.  
2. No radiation, no motion, no external fields.  
3. The causal deformation is time-independent.  
4. The phenomenal domain inherits its geometry from the causal domain.  
5. No derivatives with respect to time appear in this experiment.  

---

## Implications

- The q-profile is static.  
- The w-profile is static.  
- The mapping from q to physical radius \( r \) is static.  
- No quantity depends on the universal parameter \( T \).  
- No dynamics, no evolution, no flow.  
- This is the reference geometry for all later thought experiments.

## Diagram Description

A central mass is shown at the origin. Concentric shells around it represent the
static deformation of the causal domain. The phenomenal domain is drawn as a
radial 3D space surrounding the mass. Beneath or behind it, the causal domain is
shown as a higher-dimensional structure whose curvature determines the shape of
the phenomenal well.
