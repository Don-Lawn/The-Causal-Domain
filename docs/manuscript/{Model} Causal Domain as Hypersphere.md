# Causal Domain as Hypersphere

This model represents the causal domain as a closed, higher‑dimensional hypersphere.
It is the most complete and symmetric description of the parent‑field geometry.
Every gravitational well corresponds to a single meridian on this hypersphere.

The hypersphere model is not used directly for calculations; instead, it provides
the conceptual foundation from which the hypercone model is derived. The hypercone
is simply one meridian of this hypersphere, unrolled into a 2D slice.

---

## Purpose of the Hypersphere Model

- To represent the **full causal geometry** before any dimensional reduction.
- To show that all gravitational wells share the same underlying structure.
- To provide the **origin** of the q‑axis as a radial descent direction.
- To justify why the hypercone is universal: all meridians are equivalent.
- To anchor the relationship between causal symmetry and phenomenal curvature.

This model is the “parent object” from which the hypercone model inherits its form.

---

## Structure of the Hypersphere

- A closed, symmetric, higher‑dimensional surface.
- The Q‑axis corresponds to a radial descent from the pole toward the equator.
- Each meridian represents a possible gravitational well.
- All meridians are equivalent by symmetry.
- The geometry is static for a static mass.

No time dependence is assumed in this model.

---

## Relation to the Hypercone

A single meridian of the hypersphere can be:

1. **Selected**  
   (representing one gravitational well)

2. **Flattened / unrolled**  
   (producing a 2D hypercone slice)

3. **Normalised**  
   (yielding the q–w coordinate system)

The hypercone model is therefore a *localised, operational* representation of the
global hypersphere geometry.

## Domain Separation

### Causal Domain (k-domain)
- The hypersphere exists entirely in the causal domain.
- Contains Q, stiffness \( S(q) \), Tempo \( \tau(q) \), and the parent-field geometry.
- Not directly observable.

### Phenomenal Domain (x-domain)
- Not represented directly in this model.
- The phenomenal domain appears only after projection from the hypercone.
